READING: compare against the read-only 30-port line at the top.  These

================================ WHAT THIS MEANS ==============================

W1 write-only == read-only, to 0.01%.  30 ports: 288.0 GB/s in 1,600,019
cycles against the read sweep's 1,600,175 for the same beats.  A write beat
costs exactly what a read beat costs.  This was arithmetically guaranteed and
is recorded as a working write path, not as a finding.

W2 IS THE FINDING, and it is the first measurement on this card where the DRAM
rather than the fabric clock sets the answer.  One port doing reads AND writes
into one pseudo-channel delivers 11.77 GB/s.  It demanded 19.2 (AXI's two
directions are independent, 32 B/cycle each) from a channel that supplies 14.4.

    11.77 / 14.4 = 81.8%   <- the read/write bus turnaround, tWTR/tRTW

That is the whole cost, because at ONE port there is no second master to
arbitrate against.  It scales exactly linearly to 30 ports (353.0 GB/s
= 30 x 11.77), so nothing upstream saturates.

W3 SEPARATES TURNAROUND FROM ARBITRATION, and arbitration is FREE.  Twenty
readers and ten writers on INDEPENDENT channels: 288.0 GB/s in 1,600,176
cycles, against read-only's 1,600,175.  Identical to within one cycle in 1.6
million.  The HBM switch mixes read and write masters at no cost whatsoever;
100% of W2's 18.2% loss is the DRAM bus turning around.

353.0 GB/s IS THE HIGHEST FIGURE EVER MEASURED ON THIS CARD, 22.6% above the
288.0 read-only number, because bidirectional traffic uses both AXI directions
per port.  It does NOT raise subsystem A's supply: A's weight stream is
read-only and still gets 32 B/cycle/port = 9.6 GB/s.  Quote 288 for read-only
workloads and 353 only for genuinely bidirectional ones.

COUNTERINTUITIVE CONSEQUENCE FOR B (see B section 3.4).  B's state sweep is an
in-place read-modify-write, so it turns the bus around continuously, and the
obvious fix was a ping-pong between two state regions to separate reads from
writes.  MEASUREMENT SAYS DO NOT.  At LANES=32 B needs 19.2 GB/s each way:

    in-place, 4 ports doing R+W : 47.1 GB/s (23.5 each way)  +23% margin
    ping-pong, 2 read + 2 write : 38.4 GB/s (19.2 each way)    0% margin

The turnaround penalty is real, and it is still cheaper than dedicating each
port to one direction and leaving the other direction idle.  Both need 4 ports;
in-place has margin and needs no second copy of the state.  The ping-pong is
withdrawn, and it would have cost 37.75 MB per card to make things worse.

NOT MEASURED, do not infer:
  - Read/write RATIOS other than 1:1.  W2 issues equal beats each way.  C's
    2R+1W on ONE channel is a different mix and is not this number.
  - Turnaround at burst lengths other than ARLEN=15 (16 beats, the AXI3 max).
    Shorter bursts turn the bus around more often and must be worse.
  - Anything at 350 MHz.  This build closed at WNS +0.017 ns; there is no
    headroom to raise the clock without re-closing timing.
