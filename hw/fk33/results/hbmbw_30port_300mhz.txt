30 PORTS, BOTH STACKS, 300 MHz.  2026-08-25.  Build WNS +0.101 ns.

HEADLINE: 288.0 GB/s, 100.0% of the arithmetic ceiling, 96,000,000 beats,
zero non-OKAY read responses, exact beat counts at every point.

Sweep A scales perfectly linearly to 30 ports and the CYCLE COUNT STAYS AT
3.2 M throughout.  That is the result worth keeping: 30 pseudo-channels across
BOTH stacks run fully in parallel with no measurable interference.  Crossing to
the second stack could plausibly have introduced contention through shared
infrastructure and it introduced none.

Sweep B (oversubscription, all ports -> one channel) is flat at 9.60 GB/s from
1 port through 30, unchanged from the 15-port run.  9.60 = 32 B x 300 MHz.
Confirms again that the per-channel ACCESS PATH is 32 B/cycle regardless of how
many masters target it, so HBM's DRAM-level rate stays unobservable from the
fabric.  See docs/debugging/2026-08-25_hbm-upper-bound-is-the-clock.md.

WHAT THIS IS: usable read bandwidth at 30 of 32 SAXI ports.  SAXI_00 and
SAXI_16 carry jtag_hbm, one per stack, and that is the host's path to the
card -- so 32 ports is not available to this instrument at any clock, and
30 x 32 B x f is its true ceiling.

  288.0 GB/s  =  30 ports x 32 B x 300 MHz          MEASURED
  336.0 GB/s  =  30 ports x 32 B x 350 MHz          if 350 ever closes
  460.8 GB/s  =  32 ports x 32 B x 450 MHz          the device paper figure

So this is 62.5% of the device's paper number, and the entire remaining gap is
two ports we cannot have plus a clock we have not closed.  Nothing here
suggests the memory is the limit.

STILL NOT COVERED: writes.  hbm_tg has no write channel at all, so C's 2R+1W
and B's four-master patterns remain untested by any of this.
