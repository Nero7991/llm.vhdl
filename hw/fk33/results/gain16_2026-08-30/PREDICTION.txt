THE MODEL I AM BETTING ON, and it CONTRADICTS the two estimates in my brief.

TRACK GWTWO's section 7 derived "14-bit -> ~118 tiles" and "11-bit codebook
index -> ~93 tiles" by dividing bits-per-element into RAMB36 capacity at the
96.3% packing efficiency it measured at 16 bits.  I think that arithmetic is
the wrong shape, for the same reason GWTWO's own registered prediction died:
Vivado does not pack to a capacity, it picks a NATIVE ASPECT RATIO and then
tiles depth.

DERIVED.  The ROM is 266,240 deep.  A RAMB36 holds 36,864 bits and offers
32Kx1, 16Kx2, 8Kx4, 4Kx9, 2Kx18, 1Kx36, 512x72.  Tiles needed to reach depth
266,240 at each mode:

    w = 1        ceil(266240/32768) =  9
    w = 2        ceil(266240/16384) = 17
    w = 3..4     ceil(266240/ 8192) = 33
    w = 5..9     ceil(266240/ 4096) = 65
    w = 10..18   ceil(266240/ 2048) = 130

So the cost is a STEP FUNCTION OF WIDTH, not a linear function of bits.  Every
width from 10 to 18 costs the same 130, and the whole saving lives at the
w = 9 step.  MEASURED head is 135 = 130 + 5, so there is about 5 tiles of
overhead on top of the band figure.

PREDICTED, with the +5 overhead carried:

    head      135   (reproduces GWTWO gw1 exactly, or the harness is wrong)
    sw16      135   (the rewriter at 16 bits is a semantic no-op)
    sw14      135   NO SAVING -- the naive estimate says 118
    sw11      135   NO SAVING -- the naive estimate says 93 for the codebook
    sw9        65..75   THE STEP.  This is where the tiles are.
    sw9_4_1   105..115  = 65 + 33 + 9 + overhead

FALSIFIERS, named now so the answer only has to be classified:

  F1  sw14 below 125 tiles.  Then width is not banded and the naive
      bits/capacity arithmetic is closer to right than this model.
  F2  sw9 above 125 tiles.  Then Vivado does not use the 4Kx9 mode for a
      9-bit ROM and NO width lever exists at all; the honest report is that
      the 16 tiles cannot be bought here.
  F3  sw9_4_1 at or above sw14.  Then Vivado does not honour a hand-split
      store, and splitting is dead.
  F4  DSP moves off 41 at any point.  Nothing here should touch a multiplier;
      if one appears the lever is a regression whatever it does to BRAM.

WHAT THIS PREDICTION IS NOT.  sw11 and sw9 are LOSSY probes.  They measure the
COST of a width, not a shippable design.  If sw9 is cheap it still has to be
reached by a codebook, and the codebook's own table and decode are extra.
